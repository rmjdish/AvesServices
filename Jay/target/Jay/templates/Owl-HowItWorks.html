How OWL and Condor work together

OWL is where you discover variables. Condor is where you finalise and submit a request. Here's exactly how the two connect.

Two ways to complete the dictionary

The Data Dictionary is just an Excel spreadsheet, marked Y against every variable you want. You can complete it entirely by hand — open a blank copy and mark variables yourself — or build your dictionary on OWL and let it fill itself in for you automatically.

Why OWL is the better route

OWL has search tools this manual route doesn't: keyword search across the whole dictionary, browse-by-category, and — usefully — searchable documentation, so you can find a variable from a write-up or topsheet rather than already knowing its name. It's the faster, more reliable way to build an accurate list.

Uploading to Condor: Upload Data Dictionary

Whichever way you completed the dictionary — downloaded from OWL, or filled in by hand — upload the same file using Upload Data Dictionary from the OWL menu. The file is checked before anything is added:

  • Missing variable name — rows marked Y with no variable name are skipped and listed by row number.
  • Invalid Request variable value — anything other than blank or exactly Y (a lowercase y, N, or a stray note) is skipped and listed with the value found.
  • Duplicate variable names — only the first occurrence marked Y is kept; the rest are listed.

Once you confirm, the variables are added to your Condor basket, and you're taken straight to a results page showing exactly what happened — including anything that couldn't be added because it's restricted. That check needs Condor's own records to answer, so it only appears at this point, rather than before you confirm. If your data dictionary takes your basket over 500 variables, Condor handles that automatically when you save — see below.

A note on restricted variables

Restricted variables can be added to your data dictionary on OWL and downloaded like any other — OWL doesn't block them. But when that file is uploaded to Condor, restricted variables are not imported automatically with the rest of your request — you'll see them listed on the results page once you confirm, not before. Each one needs to be requested separately; who to contact depends on the specific variable, and that contact is shown when you hover the lock icon next to it on OWL. Check your data dictionary for lock icons before uploading, so you're not waiting on a second round trip after submission.

Large data dictionaries: automatic splitting

You no longer need to split a large list yourself before uploading. If your data dictionary has more than 500 variables, Condor automatically divides it into several saved baskets when you save — each with no more than 500 — so you can upload the whole thing in one go.

Important: how split baskets are reviewed

If your request has been split into multiple saved baskets, they are reviewed collectively, as one overall request — not as separate, independent submissions. Requests for a large number of variables may take longer to process, since they go through a more thorough risk assessment, which can delay your dataset being created. You may be asked to justify your request if your total comes to more than 500 variables across a single submission, even though it's been split into several saved baskets.
